    Fir Bolg

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 11:36:07 about 3 minutes

    place Group artifact Person hero group mythological figure Text character Place Fir Bolg Book of Invasions Nemed Tuatha De Danann Eochaid mac Eirc Tailtiu Nuada Aran Islands Dela Belgae Fir ocean Mag Tuired the Book of the Tell Bolg

    The Fir Bolg are a legendary race of Ireland, remembered in the Book of the Taking of Ireland. The tale tells how the island was settled in waves, and the Fir Bolg are one of those peoples. Older reading set their name beside a word for a bag and called them the men of the bags. More recent study links the name to the Builg or the Belgae, the Celtic tribes that gave Belgium its name. Others say the people trace their line to a god named Bolg, so the name could mean the sons of the god Bolg. They are counted among the children of Nemed and came after their kin were driven out. One of the line reached the land of the Greeks and stayed long in that land, and the memory of that stay is kept in the idea of the bag. They returned to the island, and their chiefs divided the realm among their sons. One family ruled the shore that faces the sea, and the whole of the country was shared out between the children. They held the kingship and taught that the tribes of the west waned no part in their count. After long the Tuatha Dé Dan came into the island, and the Fir Bolg met them at Mag Tuired. In that battle the king of the new gods was struck and lost an arm, and the Fir Bolg were thrown and lost the thousand. Some of the tribe then drew aside to the sea islands and to the far corners of the land, and their name clings to the rocky forts and the pebbled plains. The Fir Domnann are a kindred people of the same stamp, who stood with the Fir Bolg in the old lists of the tribes.
