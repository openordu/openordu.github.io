    Ballads and narrative songs

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 11:36:07 about 2 minutes

    hero text figure Oisean Fionn Cú Chulainn Oscar Diarmaid Goll mac Morna Book of the Dean of Lismore Ulster Cycle

    Ballads and narrative songs are a central form of Gaelic verse. Oisean, the sole survivor of Fionn's companions, speaks with Saint Patrick about past glory and heroic deeds. The frame repeats the old Irish dialogue of survivors and saints. A few songs follow the Ulster Cycle and place the warrior Cú Chulainn at the heart of the action. Songs of grief for slain fighters hold a strong place in the group. Three pieces mourn the death of Oscar, the grandson of Fionn. The lay of Diarmaid records how Fionn caused the fall of his own nephew. A rival band led by Goll mac Morna often closes with the Fianna. Tales of Norse raiders appear in these verses, but the time of the hero makes the claim a false note. Ireland and Scotland first share the tradition. The verse patterns split as the spoken tongue changes. Lismore's dean kept the oldest Scottish songs in his manuscript. The learned men of the 1700s recorded the folk of Perthshire and Argyll.
