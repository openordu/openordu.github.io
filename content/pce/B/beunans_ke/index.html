    Beunans Ke

    [, , ', C, h, r, i, s, t, o, p, h, e, r, , G, o, d, w, i, n, ', , ] 2026-08-28 09:17:37 about 3 minutes

    work place text figure Beunans Ke St Ke Middle Cornish Graham Thomas National Library of Wales Geoffrey of Monmouth Truro Beunans Meriasek

    Beunans Ke is a saint's play that sets out the life of the Cornish holy man St Ke. St Ke also answers to the name Kea. The verse belongs to the Middle Cornish tradition and was probably composed about the year 1500. For a very long time readers did not know the work at all. It fell to Graham Thomas to reveal it. The loose manuscript first showed up inside the papers of J. E. Caerwyn Williams. Those papers passed to the National Library of Wales, given over by the scholar's widow. A printed note from Thomas followed in 2002 inside an academic journal. The library has since cleaned and rebound the volume, and it remains there for study. No one can say where the text first came from. The copy we have is partial and stands in the hand of the later 1500s. It stays incomplete, since the start, the close, and some inner foils are all gone. For a while people held that the bundle carried two separate works. One part sets out the saint's life. The other part, in later foils, gives an Arthurian episode that never mentions the saint. That portion traces the version told in Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae. The fullest account of St Ke lives in the book by Albert Le Grain. He translated a Latin life that is now lost. The broad parish of Kea, near Truro in Cornwall, forms the Cornish scene. The play's manuscript calls the hero Ke, where the Latin form is Keladocus, and the Breton sources write Ke Coloc. The Cornish style is strong and stands well beside Beunans Meriasek. The opening stays lively and free, while the Arthurian part grows heavier. Readers now treat the two parts as a single play.

    • Beunans Ke: A Middle Cornish saint's play about St Ke, composed about 1500.

    • St Ke: Cornish holy figure also named Kea who lends his name to the parish.

    • National Library of Wales: Institution that accepted the donated manuscript and saw it cleaned and rebuilt.

    • Geoffrey of Monmouth: Author of the Historia Regum Britanniae, mirrored in the Arthurian part of the play.
