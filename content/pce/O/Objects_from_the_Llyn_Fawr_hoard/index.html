    Llyn Fawr hoard

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 11:36:08 about 2 minutes

    Place concept Group Llyn Fawr Morgannwg Cymru Abertawe Hallstatt C National Museum of Wales

    Llyn Fawr is an archaeological site in Glamorgan (Morgannwg), south Wales (Cymru), about 18 miles north-east of Swansea (Abertawe). Workers recovered the Llyn Fawr hoard when the peaty mountain tarn drained during the building of a reservoir between 1910 and 1911. O. G. S. Crawford and R. E. M. Wheeler first presented the find in 1921. Later finds, recovered in the same years, reached the National Museum of Wales in 1936. These included a second bronze vessel and a piece of a Hallstatt C iron sword. The hoard holds 24 objects in total: two bronze cauldrons, six socketed bronze axes, three socketed bronze gouges, two winged chapes, a bronze razor, a bronze belt-clasp, an ornamental bronze plate, three bronze discs, two bronze sickles, an iron sickle, an iron spearhead, and an iron sword. The first collection makes up a unitary deposit spanning the closing Bronze Age and the opening Iron Age, around 650 bc. The two bronze cauldrons belong to class B1, with close parallels in Ireland (Ériu), evidencing Atlantic trade. The iron sickle, similar to Irish bronze examples, shows that iron production had arrived while bronze models were still imitated. The Hallstatt C iron sword stands as an import. The name Llyn Fawr means 'large pool' in Welsh, the word llyn, originally neuter, generally feminine in south Wales.
