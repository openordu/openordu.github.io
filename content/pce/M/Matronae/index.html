    Matronae

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 08:44:05 about 2 minutes

    group person Concept Person Matronae Deae Matres maiden crone mother goddesses fertility Aufaniae Vacallinehae Matrona Matres Domesticae

    Matronae is the Latin plural name of a group of mother goddesses. Koch counts 84 instances of the name in Roman-period dedications. The cult stood firm in the Rhineland, near Bonn and Cologne. There the goddesses carried the byname Aufaniae. Another shrine at Pesch kept the name Vacallinehae. The singular Matrona is also the old name of the Marne river. So one figure holds the name of a stream and its divine woman at once. The cult grew strongest around the Marne and spread west toward Britain. On later inscriptions the Mothers appear as a triad. They hold cornucopia, fruit, and other signs of plenty. They often nurse a baby with one breast bared. In Roman Britain the home goddesses, the Domesticae, gained dedications at York and at forts on the northern wall. The great count of images and shrines shows how central these mothers were.

    • Matronae: the Latin name of the Deae Matres, the Divine Mothers of the Continental Celts

    • Matronae: usually shown as three seated figures of maiden, mother, and crone

    • Matronae: goddesses of abundance and of the fertility of the land

    • Matronae: cult held firm in the Rhineland under the byname Aufaniae

    • Matronae: the plural of a river-god name that becomes the Marne

    • Matronae: triadic images with cornucopia and a bare-breasted mother

    • Matronae: the domestic Mothers named in Roman-British dedications
