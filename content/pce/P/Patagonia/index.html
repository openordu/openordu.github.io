    Patagonia

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:20:28 about 2 minutes

    Place Person Group Patagonia Chubut Y Wladfa Michael D. Jones Gaiman Cymry

    Patagonia is the setting of Y Wladfa. This is the Welsh colony in Chubut, Argentina. It stands out as the one place where the Welsh language enjoys greater daily use than English. Michael D. Jones championed the plan to plant a colony. He later headed the Congregational Theological College at Bala. He wanted a settlement able to hold off assimilation. The Welsh Colony Movement fixed on Patagonia for its New Wales. The roomy land drew it. So did the stretch of empty country far from other European settlement. After a voyage of three months, 153 Welsh people came ashore at New Bay. They landed on the twenty-eighth of July 1865. The start was bitter, with crops that failed. By 1867 the numbers had sunk to 116. Still the hope of a Welsh-speaking community held firm. Within a decade or so of the 1880s the colony was plainly thriving. The whole Chubut valley lay settled. Out of bare ground the settlers raised fields and orchards. They went on to raise farms, chapels, and towns. A network of roads and a railway also took shape. A local government ran its business wholly in Welsh. In 1904 the Intermediate School of Gaiman opened. In 1896 the Argentine state took over the schools. It drove a policy of Spanish only. By the 1970s most Welsh speakers left were over forty. From the 1980s feeling turned. In 1997 the Welsh Office paid for teachers of Welsh in Chubut. The project carried on after its first three years.
