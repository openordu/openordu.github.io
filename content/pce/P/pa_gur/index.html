    Pa Gur yv y Porthaur?

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 08:44:05 about 3 minutes

    text hero Custom Group Manuscript Place Creature figure Pa Gur Arthur Cai Modron Kulhwch Black Book of Carmarthen Mari Lwyd Cath Paluc Celliwg Gododdin Minit Eidin

    Pa Gur yv y Porthaur?, which asks what man is the porter, is a short Middle Welsh poem whose only copy sits inside the Black Book of Carmarthen, a codex of the early thirteenth century. The title is the challenge that Arthur calls out at the gate of a court. The poem works as an early catalogue of Arthurian champions and their deeds. It keeps company with the triads, Culhwch ac Olwen, and the Englynion y Beddau as a small store of older lore. The heroes named are Kei (Cai), Bedwyr, and the son of the mother Modron. Mabon son of Mellt, Manawydan son of Llŷr, with Uthr Bendragon, the father of Arthur, also appearing. The verses tell of a fight at Kelli, which later readers link to Arthur's court at Celliwig, and of a battle with the monstrous cat Cath Paluc. Arthur also clashes with the Cinbin, a dog-headed people whose image the Irish texts keep in the conchinn. The place of that encounter, Minit Eidin, is the Mount of Edinburgh, capital of the Gododdin. The formulaic words at the door echo a Welsh Christmas custom, the Mari Lwyd, in which a horse-skull is sung to at the threshold. Culhwch ac Olwen frames its own heroes inside a guarded court, the reverse of the poem, where Arthur must seek entry. One manuscript, the Black Book of Carmarthen, holds the only copy. A later frame tale courts the giant's daughter, which the poem itself does not include.
