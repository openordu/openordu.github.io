    Dialog etre Arzur ha Guynglaff

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:26:47 about 3 minutes

    text hero figure concept people Dialog etre Arzur ha Guynglaff Arthur Guynglaff Bretons Middle Breton Myrddin Merlin Roue Stefan

    Koch gives the piece its full name in the Breton tongue. It dates the poem to about 1450, citing Piette and Fleuriot. Only poor copies from the 18th century survive today. One copy from 1710 follows a manuscript made in 1619. The text holds two hundred and forty-seven lines in a corrupt form. In the tale Arthur catches the prophet Guynglaff and presses his speech. Guynglaff then tells Arthur what would come before the world ends. The lines name the years 1470 through 1476 and again 1486 through 1488. They also name wars against the English and the ruin they spread. Most of these events never came, so the piece was written before 1470. It rests on hope of what the future might bring. Later lines point to events of the 16th century. Fleuriot reads Guynglaff as a face of the Brittonic seer Myrddin. The song links the Arthurian seer Merlin with the later prophet Roue Stefan. Piriou called the name a blessed sick man, tied to epilepsy. This is the oldest Arthurian writing that survives in the Breton tongue. The Dialog etre Arzur ha Guynglaff is a verse piece in Middle Breton. Its full title names its two speakers. Arthur leads the exchange as a ruler of the Bretons. Guynglaff answers him across the whole talk. The piece works in the manner of a prophecy. It belongs to a set of Middle Breton sacred verse. That set includes later religious poems of the region. Within this body the dialogue ranks as the leading work. Its standing gives it a special worth. It gives rare proof of prophecy verse in Breton. The piece holds a central place in the Breton record.
