    Dagda

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 08:44:05 about 2 minutes

    Deity Group place epithet literary text Dagda Tuatha Dé Danann Mórrígan Bóand Brigit Aonghus óg Cath Maige Tuired Eochaid Ollathair Brú na Bóinne

    The Dagda is an old god of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Ireland's supernatural people. His name means the good god, which in this case points to skill and fine work rather than charm. He serves as artisan, warrior, diviner, farmer, and king inside one body. He owns a cauldron that never empties, and he drags a huge club mounted on a cart. His pigs cook and come back to life, and his orchard bears fruit in every season. The Fomoiri mock him as gross and greedy. They pour porridge into a pit and order him to eat it, and he clears it to the last scrape of his finger. Yet they misjudge him. He is a master of craft and magic whose powers span death and rebirth in the same stroke. His title Eochaid Ollathair, the father of all, is often compared with the Norse All-Father Odin. He sires the god of youth and beauty, Oengus; the goddess Brigit, whom we also call Bríg; and sons named Áed, Bodb Derg, and others.
