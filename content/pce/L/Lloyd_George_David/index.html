    Lloyd George, David

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:20:28 about 3 minutes

    Person Group Place Lloyd George, David Wales Caernarfonshire Cymru Fydd Ireland Northern Ireland

    Lloyd George, David, later the 1st Earl Lloyd-George of Dwyfor (1863-1945), was born in Manchester and raised at Llanystumdwy in Caernarfonshire. He was a native speaker of Welsh and served as British Prime Minister from 1916 to 1922. He trained as a solicitor and qualified in 1885, then in 1888 won a place as an alderman on the Caernarfonshire County Council. In 1890 a by-election returned him as Liberal MP for the Caernarfon Boroughs, and he quickly earned a voice on Welsh subjects such as the disestablishment of the Anglican church in Wales and the rise of Welsh national feeling. He belonged to the Cymru Fydd (Young Wales) movement, but when that effort collapsed he turned away from purely Welsh questions toward the politics of all Britain. He made his name opposing the South African War and later the education measure promoted by Balfour in 1902, then entered the cabinet in 1905. As Chancellor he pushed forward sweeping social welfare and a campaign against the House of Lords through his famous 'People's Budget'. Although at first unsure about the war, he held the munitions ministry from May 1915, served as War Secretary during 1916, and finally took the premiership that December, showing firm resolve in a difficult hour. Re-elected in December 1918, he led a Conservative-dominated coalition that handled a crowded set of domestic and foreign burdens and negotiated with Irish leaders so that a separate Ireland and Northern Ireland came into being in 1921-22. His coalition fell in October 1922, and he left office facing long years of mistrust among Liberals and the public. He returned as Liberal leader in 1926 and threw himself into the 1929 campaign, then into a sophisticated plan for Bangor, but neither succeeded. In 1940 he helped press Neville Chamberlain out of the premiership, and after accepting an earldom early in 1945 he died at Cricieth that March. His long career left a deep mark on Wales and on the Cymru sense of identity throughout twentieth-century political life.
