    Llyfr Du O'r Waun

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:20:28 about 2 minutes

    Artefact Person Text Place Llyfr Du O'r Waun Welsh law Iorwerth redaction J. Gwenogvry Evans Denbighshire

    The Llyfr Du O'r Waun, the Black Book of Chirk, is a medieval Welsh manuscript. It ranks among the most interesting manuscripts of medieval Welsh law. It carries an incomplete version of the Welsh laws in their Iorwerth redaction, part of the wider Cyfraith Hywel. In Aneurin Owen's Ancient Laws the book bears the siglum A. Its title, which ties it to Chirk in Denbighshire, appears to date no earlier than the sixteenth century. J. Gwenogvryn Evans granted the book a central place in the field. He claimed the book held the oldest law text to survive, dating it first to 1200 and later revising this to 1220. It was also the first law manuscript published in full, with a facsimile issued by Gwenogvryn Evans in 1909. Timothy Lewis brought out a diplomatic edition, and his law glossary drew heavily on the same source. Later scholarship moved the book toward the margins of the subject. It is unlikely that the manuscript was copied before 1250, and its text now appears to be one branch of a wider tradition rather than the archetype of the Iorwerth redaction. Six scribes contributed to its making, and the writing points to a legal rather than a church-based scriptorium.

    • Llyfr Du O'r Waun: is a medieval Welsh manuscript known as the Black Book of Chirk and holds an incomplete copy of the Iorwerth redaction of the laws of Cyfraith Hywel

    • Llyfr Du O'r Waun: was promoted by J. Gwenogvry Evans, who dated it to the years around 1200 to 1220 and published it as the first complete facsimile

    • Llyfr Du O'r Waun: was copied by about six scribes and was probably produced by a legal school in north-west Wales
