    Apollo

    [, , ', C, h, r, i, s, t, o, p, h, e, r, , G, o, d, w, i, n, ', , ] 2026-08-28 11:28:09 about 3 minutes

    Apollo is a Romano-Celtic god. The Roman legions had orders to bring conquered lands into the empire. One method was the interpretatio romana. Under it, local divinities received Latin names. The Roman pantheon met a Celtic polytheism so extreme that the generic oath was by the gods of the people. The legions renamed many gods and gave dozens the name Apollo. Apollo was not a Roman name at first. The Romans adopted it from the Greeks, for whom Apollo was a sun deity. These Celtic Apollos had little to do with the Greek original. As a god of healing and of light, Apollo was used to rename the deities of hot springs. The Celts used these springs as healing spas. Several regional Apollos are recorded. Apollo Amarcolitanus was the god of the distant gaze. Apollo Anextiomarus was a Continental god of healing or protection. Apollo Atepomarus was the god of horses and the sun, linked to Cernunnos and to Belenus. Apollo Canumagus, a British god, connected with dogs. Apollo Grannus healed the springs across Europe and Scotland, with the consort Sirona. He draws his name from a Gaulish word for heat or light. His healing springs stood at Aachen and in the Vosges, and the Roman historian Dio Cassius wrote that the emperor Caracalla sought a cure from him. Apollo Moritasgus was a healing god of France, consort of Damona. Apollo Vindonnus healed eye diseases. MacKillop also notes the epithet Citharoedus, which marks Apollo as a player of the lyre. A stele at Reims pairs Apollo with the antlered god Cernunnos. In Gaul his cult often merged a native solar and healing power with the form of the Graeco-Roman deity.
