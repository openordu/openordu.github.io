    Griffiths, Ann

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-28 08:44:05 about 3 minutes

    Person Place Ann Griffiths Thomas Griffiths Ruth Evans John Hughes Benjamin Jones Wales

    Griffiths, Ann (1776-1805) became famous largely after her death, when she came to be counted among the leading hymn writers that Wales has produced and its most acclaimed female poet. She was born Ann Thomas at the farmhouse of Dolwar Fach in a parish of Montgomeryshire, to a family that was reasonably well off and Anglican at first but went over to Welsh Nonconformism at the end of the seventeen hundreds. The farm by 1798 served as a gathering for local Calvinist Methodists and was registered for that purpose in 1803. Griffiths underwent a conversion in 1796 after she heard preaching by Benjamin Jones of Pwllisi, and it seems she began composing verses from around that same period. She almost never wrote her songs down, treating them as private fervent devotions meant to her own use rather than for the congregation, and that is why they cannot be dated with precision. They were preserved by an unlettered household maid, Ruth Evans, who kept them and later rendered them to her husband, a Methodist preacher named John Hunt, who entered them into his notebook. From there they eventually reached Thomas Charles, who made room for some in the hymn collection that came out in 1806. By then Ann Griffiths had already died, shortly after the birth of a child, and her wedding to a neighbour farmer, Thomas Griffiths, came about in 1804. The power of feeling in her verses and the marrow in their devotion granted them quick success with congregations, and later writers of the 20th century chose to place her among the best of all Christian mystics.
