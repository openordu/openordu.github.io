    superstition

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:26:47 about 3 minutes

    Being Concept superstition horse Banshee fairy Evil Eye Rowan Corpse Candle Corn Dolly Witch Second Sight

    Superstition is the leftover habit of an older faith. When one religion gives way to another, the old one does not leave the field at once. Its practices stay, but the old meaning fades, so people call the leftover superstition. Such belief reaches across the whole of life, from the small round of the home to the great turn of the seasons. Some beliefs stay silent, some find a place in the daily act, and many attach a custom to a need. The Celtic countries keep a rich web of this folklore. In Ireland a new house was sited so it did not shut the way the fairies used to walk. Farmers bound the herd's tails with lengths of red ribbon as a guard. A witch could slip into the form of a hare, and the evil eye could strike a man without warning. A red-haired woman met on the road to market or to the boat could bring bad luck to a trip. Rowan hung above the door kept out harm, and forge water eased the ache of the joints. The boundary of death had its own signs. The corpse candle and the death bird told a home that an end was near, and a wake kept the dead from troubling the living. In the new year the first guest over the door, the first-footer, carried either luck or ruin for the house. The last sheaf of harvest, saved as a figure called the caseg fedi, held back bad luck until the next crop. These traditions mark the turning year and bind a people to its past.
