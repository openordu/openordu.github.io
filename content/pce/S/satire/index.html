    satire

    [, , ', C, h, r, i, s, t, o, p, h, e, r, , G, o, d, w, i, n, ', , ] 2026-08-28 11:28:10 about 3 minutes

    Satire was a potent ritual speech in early Ireland, most often delivered in verse. Many tales treat its use and abuse. Cath Maige Tuired and the Táin Bó Cúailnge do. So does the tale of Néide and Caíer in Sanas Chormaic. The law text Bretha Nemed discusses the powers of the poets, and a tract called Cis lir fodla aíre? lists the many kinds of satire. The sources mark three differences. A satire could be public or private. It could be prose or verse. It might name its victim clearly or leave him vague. The craft rested on skill with language, since the worth of the barb lay in the value of its verse.

    The most formal satire was a set ritual. The poet cut a rod in the shape of a cross. He carved a cross on the first arm. The offence went on the second, the offender's name on the third, and praise on the fourth. He set the rod in the earth as a three-stage rite meant to force compensation. He could not act too soon without losing his claim. But if the offender refused terms by the set time, the poet could satirize him freely. The trefhocal, a poem that mixed praise and blame, mirrored the offender's fall from honour to shame. Such verse echoed the legal process of distraint, and one example survives that threatened the Fir Arddae.

    Much satire was common and informal, short songs begging for food or lodging. The learned filid tried to keep the craft for the educated. They spoke scornfully of the low-born cáinti who used it to seize wealth. Satire was open to women too; female satirists recur in the literature and used it to right wrongs. Yet the chief office of satire was public. A ruler who fed his poets poorly could be named before the people, and so the tribe made him answer. Cairpre mac Étoíne satirized Bres for his miserly hospitality. His words aired the king's injustice. The Tuath Dé then rose and threw off his reign. Satire thus guarded hospitality and kept the strong to the common standard.
