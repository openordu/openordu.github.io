    Colman mac Leneni

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:20:28 about 2 minutes

    Language Person Place Colmán mac Lénéni Cloyne Old Irish Gaelic

    Colman mac Leneni, fully Colmán mac Lénéni, was a high-ranking Irish poet who died on 14 November 604. Scholars rank him among the earliest securely dated Gaelic poets. He founded and likely led the monastery of Cluain Uama at Cloyne in Munster. He was called an aithlaec, a word for a man who took up religion in mid-life. Saint Brenainn of Clonfert is said to have swayed him toward this calling. Later stories claim he set aside poetry after entering the monastery, yet the surviving verses show he kept writing Irish poetry until death. Seven fragments of his work survive, studied by Thurneysen and later by Liam Breatnach. His language is archaic Old Irish, with some terms that soon grew obsolete. Stylistically his verse stands between classical Old Irish syllabic verse and the free-form roscada. The fragments reveal a master of compact, telegraphic metaphor already shaped to a Christian setting.
