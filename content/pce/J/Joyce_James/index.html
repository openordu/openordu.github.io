    Joyce, James

    Christopher Godwin 2026-08-27 05:20:28 about 2 minutes

    Person Work Place Joyce, James Dublin Rathgar Trieste Paris Nora Barnacle Ulysses Finnegans Wake Dubliners

    Joyce, James Augustine — writing early as 'James Augustine Aloysius Joyce', the Irish modernist who died at Zürich in 1941 — came from Rathgar in Dublin where he was born in 1882. His middle-class Catholic family grew poorer because of his father's drinking, yet Jesuits schooled the young Joyce at Clongowes Wood, Belvedere and University College. In 1904 he sailed away with Nora Barnacle, who became his lifelong partner, settling first in Trieste and then, from 1920, in Paris. His volume Dubliners (1914) recalled the city he left; Ulysses (1922) followed one day in Dublin during June 1904 and won him international fame. The Wake (1939), locally titled Finnegan, closed his use of invented, layered language. Once a Parnellite, he turned against the Celtic revival, judging it sentimental and friendly to imperial views of Irish life. Repeated eye surgery left him nearly blind before he died of a duodenal ulcer in 1941.

    • Joyce, James: was born in Rathgar, Dublin, in 1882 into a declining middle-class Catholic family

    • Joyce, James: was educated by Jesuits at Clongowes Wood, Belvedere and University College

    • Joyce, James: left Dublin with Nora Barnacle in 1904 and later lived in Trieste, Paris and Zürich

    • Ulysses: is set in Dublin on 16 June 1904 and secured Joyce's literary reputation

    • Joyce, James: rejected the Celtic revival as sentimental folklorism aligned with British imperial views

    • Joyce, James: died of a duodenal ulcer in 1941 after years of near-blinding eye operations
