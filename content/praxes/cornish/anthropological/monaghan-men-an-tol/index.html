Men-an-Tol

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Men-an-Tol ("hole stone") is a Cornish megalithic monument: a circular holed stone with two flanking uprights. It is an ancestral and boundary work, the kind of prehistoric site that the popular mind later carried folk practice to.

The praxis turns on the rite of passage through the hole. At such holed stones the common folk practice was to pass a child (or a sick or ailing body) through the aperture as a cure — a rite of passage and healing that grafted itself onto the older megalith. The holed stone stands at the hinge of the old religion and the folk healing custom of a land ringed by such stones.
