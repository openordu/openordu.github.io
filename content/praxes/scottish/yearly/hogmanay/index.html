Hogmanay (Hogmanay)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Hogmanay is the Scottish keeping of the year's turn, the night that closes the old year and opens the new. Its name is the Scots word for the last day of the year, and it gathers the customs of cleansing and renewal that the Celtic calendar centered on the hinge of the year: the house swept and the old cleared out, the debts of the year settled, the doors made ready to receive the first-foot.

The distinctive custom is the first-footing: the first person to cross the threshold after the old year dies carries the luck of the new one, and is welcomed in with food and drink and song. Fire holds a central place, as at the great turnings of the year — the carrying of a burning brand through the community to kindle the year afresh.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The Scottish new-year eve, kept on the last night of the year.

  • First-footing and the hearth fire carry the luck of the new year.
