Vernal Equinox

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The vernal equinox is the hinge of the brightening season, when day and night stand equal before the sun begins to climb past the dark. In the historical Celtic solar calendar it is one of the natural quarter-points of the year, set between the fire festivals — the turn where the growing light gains the upper hand and the soil begins to shrug.

For the devout polytheist the observance is one of balance and of beginning: the fields readied for planting, the seed drawn and sorted, the household turned toward the working of the land. It is a quieter turning than the great fires, a hinge of the working year rather than of the sacred cycle.
