Cailleach

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The Cailleach (the "veiled one" or "hooded one") is a Scottish and Irish folkloric figure or goddess, probably descending from a pre-Celtic divinity. She was credited with forming the landscape, dropping rocks out of her apron or hurling them at an enemy. She was the winter hag: an old woman with a blue-grey countenance, sharp eyesight, and a single eye, yet possessed of strength that could best the fastest reaper in the land.

For the devout polytheist her meaning turns on the sovereignty rite. Her prime activity was the bestowal of rule on the chosen king, who typically had to kiss or lie with the Cailleach in hag form before she unveiled herself as a radiant young maid. That tale-readers have long read as an image of the land blossoming under a just ruler. In Munster she is the Hag of Beare, tied to the Beara peninsula.

The Cailleach frames the hinge of winter: an old woman born from the winter sun, growing younger through the dark season and ending as a lovely maid — an image of the year regenerating.
