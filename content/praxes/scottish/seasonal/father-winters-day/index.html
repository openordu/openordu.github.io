Father Winter's Day (Lá Fhéile Céad-tiarna)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Father Winter's Day is kept in the depths of January, when the cold has set firm and the year still stands in its dark half. In Irish lands it is tied to the name of the first lord and to the Scottish festival of the winter saint — a day that honours the person of the winter season itself rather than any single festival turning.

The observance acknowledges the severity of the cold as a power to be met rather than feared — the winter as the old man of the year who must be greeted, fed, and borne with until the spring turns him back. It is a feast of endurance and of honouring the season of hardship.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The feast of the winter person, kept in mid January at the depth of cold.

  • Keep: honour the hard season, meet the cold without fear.
