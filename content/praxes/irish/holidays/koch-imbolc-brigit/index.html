Imbolc and the goddess Brigit

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Imbolc, the first of February, opened as the milk began to flow in the ewes and the lambing season came in. Its name speaks of that turn: this was the festival of the milk that begins to flow in the fold, of the first life of spring held against the last of winter's cold. In the old Celtic framing it is tied to the goddess Brigit in her office of fertility, and she has been linked besides to a cult of fire.

Brigit stood as tutelary goddess of the Laigin, the Leinstermen, and Leinster is the very region where the ancient geographer Ptolemy placed the Brigantes tribe — the two names sharing a root. The goddess commands poets, smiths, and healers, and she governs the fertile and the fiery. Her great power survives into the Christian saint who took her name, her feast-day, and much of her office: the church's Brigit keeps poets, smiths, and healers under her care, guards livestock, and is bound to images of fire and to the abundance of the milking cow.

The line between the goddess and the saint cannot be drawn cleanly, for the living customs that surround the saint point to a deep meeting of the pre-Christian and the Christian. For the polytheist the feast keeps its older face: it is the hinge at which the ewes give milk, the forge and the healing cup are honoured, and the fire of the hearth is asked to quicken the turning year.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Mark the first of February as the feast of the milk and the ewes, the earliest turning of the year toward spring.

  • Honour Brigit in her three offices: the poet's craft, the smith's fire, and the healer's hands, keeping a gift to each.

  • Tend the household fire that day as a small act of the fire-cult, and keep a place for the cattle and the lambing-ewes in the blessing.
