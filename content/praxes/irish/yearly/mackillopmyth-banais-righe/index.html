The banais ríghe — the wedding of kingship

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The kingship of early Ireland was sealed as a marriage: the wedding of the king to the sovereignty of the land. The rite was called the banais ríghe, the wedding-feast of kingship, and in its later, more ceremonial telling the incoming king came to the bride of the land and the land was handed to him as a wife is handed. The fertility of the king was held to be bound to the fertility of the soil: a king in his strength made the fields bring forth, and the realm's good standing hung on the union of the royal house with the earth of the realm.

At its strongest the rite was a fertility business — MacKillop notes that in one telling the wedding of kingship was the supreme fertility ritual of the year, held with a greater weight than the May feast. The word feis, the feast, was originally a bedding; the banais ríghe was the marriage by which the king took the sovereignty of the land to himself in the sight of the realm, and by which the realm was renewed.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The banais ríghe is a kingship rite of the yearly shaping of the land: the union of king with sovereignty was the union of the land with its fertility.

  • Holds the making-transfer of the year: the choosing and the wedding-of-the-land renewed the whole realm.

  • Praxis: as a memory of the old wedding, renew yearly the binding of one's own household or office with the land it keeps — a eucharist of the king and of the soil kept with the four fire-feasts at the hinge of the year, and not with the invented seasonal wheel.
