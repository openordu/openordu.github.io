The fair of Tailtiu — the seasonal assembly

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The great fair of Tailtiu, the year's high assembly, was one of the marking-feasts of the Irish year and the ancestral gathering that gave the whole August festival of Lughnasa its older shape. It was held upon the stretch of ground at Teltown between Meath and Kells, and the story that gathered round it told of the founding queen Tailtiu, who was said to have cleared the plain for the grain-fields and who was buried beneath the mound where the fair was held. Each year, in her memory and in the memory of the harvest, the people of the country came together at the fair.

The assembly was not one thing but many: a market and a gathering of the festive, a place of sport and trial, of matchmaking and of the settled business of the year. The fair was open to dispute and to joy — the matchmaking at the fair of Tailtiu had its own name and was said to be briefly sealed, and the rivalries of rival wives are told of within the same fairground. What endured was the shape of an oenach, the great annual assembly at which the people of a wide countryside met at a named place once in the turning of the year, to trade and feast, to match and to make treaties, and to keep the memory of the ancestral founder whose mound the gathering honoured.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Tailtiu is the type of the Lughnasa assembly and of the oenach, the annual all-gathering of a countryside.

  • The fair gathered the practical and the sacred: trade, festivity, matchmaking and the honouring of the ancestor beneath the mound.

  • Praxis: keep an annual assembly at a named place — once in the year gather the people of the neighbourhood or kinship to trade, feast, match and remember the one who first cleared and made the place.
