Puck Fair

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Puck Fair is an Irish folk festival held in Kilorglin, County Kerry in the south-west (Munster), held on a weekend close to the harvest festival of Lughnasa. At its heart is a strange and old rite: a wild goat is seized in the hills near the town, brought down and ceremonially crowned king. Hoisted Hoisted above the town square, the "Puck" presides for three festival days that give the fair its rhythm:

  • Gathering Day,

  • Fair Day,

  • Scattering Day.

The festivities run from horse shows and outdoor games through music and drink. When the closing day ends, the animal is carried back to the hillside where it was caught and let loose once more.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Local belief dates the fair to time immemorial; some scholars read it as a relatively recent invention of perhaps three centuries. The goat does not appear as an important symbolic animal elsewhere in Celtic culture, and the word "puck" is British rather than Irish in origin (Irish: pooka), which supports the newer reading.

  • Practise it as a yearly harvest-season folk fair — observably real, tied to the Lughnasa season, and carrying the goat-crowning rite as its central act.
