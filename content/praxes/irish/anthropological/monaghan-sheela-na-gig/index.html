Sheela-na-Gig

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The Sheela-na-Gig is the figure of an exhibitionist woman — an exposed female figure — carved on Irish and some British churches and standing stones. It is one of the most commented-upon images in the Celtic and pre-Celtic sacred landscape, variously read as a fertility figure, an apotropaic guardian, or a warning against lust.

For the devout polytheist the Sheela sits in the anthropological register: a figure of the female, the generative, and the boundary, carved on the threshold of the sacred building. Her exposed form was set where everyone entering had to meet her eye — a guardian at the hinge of the church doorway.
