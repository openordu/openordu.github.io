Samhain

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Samhain was the feast of winter's incoming, held at the beginning of November (All Saints' Day, November 1, is the later Christianised marker). In the older Celtic reckoning it ranked among the four great fire feasts that divided the year. Samhain was the feast of the dead: the time when the boundary between the living world and the unseen one was held to have opened, so that the dead and the fairy folk could move among the living.

Its great assembly was the oenach held at the Hill of Tlachtga, south-west of Tara, the mythological site linked with the goddess Tlachtga. There the tribal gatherings and the ordination-ceremonies of winter's beginning took place. The season ran from Samhain at November's threshold back to summer and the Beltane fire.

The feast stood at the hinge of the year for the devout polytheist: a turning where the dead were honoured, the hearth and the household placated, and the veil between worlds acknowledged. The old fires of the year were concluded and the dark half begun.
