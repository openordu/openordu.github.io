The four festivals across the Celtic countries

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The four great festival-hinges of the old Celtic year were not an Irish possession but a shared inheritance of the whole Celtic world, each country keeping its own native name for the same turn of the season. Samain, the first of November, the autumn festival that in the reckoning of the year the darkening of the season comes first, was held at the beginning of winter. Its counterparts across the countries were Hollantide and Calan Gaeaf — the first day of winter — in Wales, Allantide in Cornwall, and Kala Goañv in Brittany. In Gaul, on the bronze Coligny calendar, the same hinge of the year was the month Samonios, a cognate word for the Irish Samain, when the Gaulish counterpart of the Roman Dis Pater was especially honoured.

Imbolc, the first of February, kept its feast of Brigit; Beltaine, the first of May, was Cyntefin and Calan Mai in Wales, Cala' Me in Cornwall and Kala-Hanv in Brittany; Lughnasa, the first of August, was the high harvest feast. The several names are the several voices of one family: the four hinges of the year were kept by every Celtic people, and each called them by its own tongue. What the dictionary gives a reader is the whole naming of the four feasts in one view — a pan-Celtic row in the praxis table.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Samain/Calan Gaeaf/Samonios (1 Nov): the year's end and beginning, when the winter quarter opens before the light.

  • Imbolc (1 Feb), Beltaine (1 May), Lughnasa (1 Aug): the other three great feasts, kept under their own names in every Celtic land.

  • Praxis: when keeping the four festivals of the year, name them in the tongue of one's own culture — Irish, Scottish, Welsh, Manx, Cornish, Breton or Gaulish — and honour the sameness of the turning across all the Celtic peoples.
