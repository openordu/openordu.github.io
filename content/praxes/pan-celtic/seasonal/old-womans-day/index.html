Old Woman's Day (Latha na Caillich)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Old Woman's Day sits in late March, at the belt of the spring turn, and its name carries over the reverence of the winter hag — the old woman of the year who must be met as the season of cold gives way. In Scottish lands she is the Cailleach, the veiled one who personifies the cold portion of the year and whose rule ends as the growing light returns.

The observance is one of passage and of greeting the change. The old woman is honoured and then turned back — met with the rites of spring, the cleansing of the house, and the acknowledgement that her grip on the year is loosening. It is a feast of the headwinter giving ground.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The feast of the winter hag at the spring turn.
