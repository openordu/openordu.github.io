Midwinter (Meán Geimhridh, Medigiamos)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Midwinter marks the still point of the Celtic solar year, the night when the sun stands at its low and the year turns back toward the light. In the stream traditions it is the solstice: the shortest day, the longest darkness, yet the turning of the year toward lengthening days. The word-set across the languages keeps the middle-of-winter meaning — Irish Meán Geimhridh, Scottish Meadhan Geamhradh, Gaulish Medigamios, Cornish Montol, Welsh Canol Gaeaf.

For the contemporary polytheist the observance is a vigil of the hinge. It is kept at the hearth: the fire that must not give out through the dark hours, the door swept and made welcome — the season the currents of the house prepare for hospitality against the cold. The feasts that follow in the fortnight — the keeping of the returning light and the nights of bread and bannock that close the year — hang directly on this still point.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Observed at the midwinter solstice, the shortest day of the historical Celtic solar year — the natural hinge of light and dark in the seasonal round.

  • Keep: a hearth vigil, the greeting of the returning light at dawn.
