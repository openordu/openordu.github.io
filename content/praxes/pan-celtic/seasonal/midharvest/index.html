Midharvest (Meán Fómhair)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Midharvest is the turn of the second harvest, kept at the autumn equinox when the day stands balanced and the gathered grain moves toward its rest. The languages hold the sense of the middle of autumn — Irish Meán Fómhair, Scottish Meadhan Foghar, Cornish Guldize, Welsh Cyhydnos yr hydref.

It stands between the height of the harvest and the season's close, the point at which the year's plenty is secured and the household turns to the storing and the settling in for the dark. For the devout polytheist it is a feast of thanksgiving and of completing — the last of the field brought in, the barn and the larder made fast.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The autumn equinox, the second hinge of the harvest season.

  • Keep: securing the yield, gratitude for the plenty gathered.
