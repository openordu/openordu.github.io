Christmas Eve (Oíche nam Bannagan)

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The eve before the winter birth holds a place of its own in the Celtic round, kept in Ireland as the night of the bannocks and in Mann as a night connected to the wren-hunting custom that follows. It belongs to the same midwinter turning as the solstice: a night of preparation, of the house set in order before the great day, of food laid ready and the hearth kept burning.

Where the midnight feast of the winter birth dominates the Christian inheritance, the underlying Celtic observance is one of provision — the bannocks baked and shared, the door set open to the traveller, the dead and the good spirits thought to walk more freely at the hinge of the year.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The eve of winterfeast, kept in Irish lands as the night of the bannocks.

  • A night of hospitality and hearth-keeping at the low of the year.
