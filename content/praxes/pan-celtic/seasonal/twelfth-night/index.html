Twelfth Night

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The twelfth night after midwinter belongs to the closing of the festival season and the turning back toward ordinary time. In the historical Celtic round it is the night when the twelve days of midwinter observance come to their end — the season's green taken down, the household turned again to the working year, yet the feast kept with its own goodbye.

In the Celtic traditions the eve and night carried the flavour of every great turning: the fire kept, the threshold made welcome, and the sense of liminality upon the hinge. It is a night of conclusion, of closing the winter hospitality and setting the house in order for the year that opens.
