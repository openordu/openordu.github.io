Lughnasa

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Lughnasa was the great autumn festival of the harvest, held at the beginning of August (also written Lughnasadh, and Christianised as Lammas). It completed the four-fire cycle, closing summer into the fall. The festival was named for Lugh, the god of many skills, and its shows and gatherings honoured him.

The god Lugh was the one who led the Tuatha Dé Danann in the great final battle and, after winning, spared the half-Fomorian king Bres in exchange for knowledge of agriculture. This link with the seasonal cycle culminates in harvest: Lugh is the harvest god, his festival the celebration of the gathered year.

The folk observance placed the season's hinge on the gathering of the wild bilberry and the berry's first fruits. At the opening of berry time the fruit is said to hold its full flavour; as the harvest festival went on, the berries were said to lose their taste. The coming of the bilberries marked the start of the Lughnasa holiday.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • The festival runs from about 1 August with hill-top gatherings, games, and the berry harvest. Observe the historical Lúnasa (August).

  • Lughnasa sits in the seasonal and holidays quadrants of the praxis matrix, across Irish, Scottish, Cornish, Breton and pan-Celtic rows.
