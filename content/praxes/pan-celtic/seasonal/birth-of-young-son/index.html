Birth of the Young Son

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

The observance of the winter birth has deep roots in the Celtic calendar as the turning point at the very depth of the year. The fixed feast on the twenty-fifth of December covers an older solar reverence: the sun, maimed and buried through the dark months, is born again and begins to climb. The Marian and derivative layers that later claimed the date name the same hinge the older tradition kept, a nativity of the new light at the low of the year.

For the devout polytheist this is a feast of regeneration. It is kept by greeting the dawn after the solstice vigil, by kindling the new fire from the last of the old, and by turning the household toward the lengthening days — the promise that the year renews what the dark has taken.
