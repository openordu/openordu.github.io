Imbolc

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Imbolc was the festival of February 1, when winter moved toward spring. It was the great feast of the goddess Brigit; the same day continues to be kept as the feast of Saint Brigit the saint, a Christian carrying of the older observance. Some old folkways never entirely died out and have been revived within recent memory.

On Imbolc the household custom centred on the making of the four-armed rush cross of Brigit and of the little rush figure known as the brídeóg ("little Brigit"). In our own day classes are held in many places for making both the rush Brigit cross and the brídeóg.

The season's customs also ran through particular regional processions:

  • The Biddy Boy processions of County Kerry, in which a group went house to house bearing an image of the saint after the fashion of a wren-day band.

  • The crios bhride (Brigit girdle) in County Galway, an article of woven straw or rushes carried around the bounds of the farmstead to bless the land and the household.

In the Cotswold hill country the old Imbolc was tied to candles that stood in for the growing light of the returning spring.
