Beltaine — the fires of Bel

Praxis Jump to heading ¶

Beltaine, the first of May, stands with Samhain as one of the two great turning-dates of the Celtic year. The word is usually taken to mean the fire of Bel, and an old Irish gloss sets the pattern: the druids made two auspicious fires with great spells and drove the cattle of the tribe between them against plague each year. That image of a living herd passing between two flames is the heart of the festival. The cattle walked through the fire to be cleansed and kept safe, and their young were assigned to the god Bel as his property.

The fire continued to matter long after the druids were gone. In the Isle of Man bonfires were raised on the hilltops at May, and their smoke was thought good for the health of cattle, crops, and people alike. The day also stood as the hinge of the working year and of the purse: labourers hired themselves on from one May Day to the next, rents fell due as gale-day, and beasts rose in value — a "wing-swarm" of bees would not come into its worth until the first of May, while the "bull-swarm" mounted in worth at Samhain.

The customs of the day turned on protection and cleansing. Young women drank dew gathered at sunrise, trusting it to clear their skin; Irish cattle were bled for their health; flowers, always the yellow ones, were gathered, and a branch or a young tree was carried home and dressed to ward the household. The fairy folk and the dead were thought most abroad on this night, and dairy food was held especially open to working wrong. Maypoles rose in many villages, and Cornwall kept the Obby Oss, the hobby horse of Padstow, on the day.

Culture notes Jump to heading ¶

  • Keep a Beltaine hearth or bonfire, and if a herd is kept, pass them between two kindled flames as an act of protection and blessing.

  • Gather the spring flowers, yellow ones above all, and set a May bough over the door as a ward.

  • On the morning of the first of May, meet the dawn with gathering, cleansing, and a turning of the household toward the season of light.
